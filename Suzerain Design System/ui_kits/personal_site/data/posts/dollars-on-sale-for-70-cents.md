Today I moved another $100k from IBKR to Polymarket to take advantage of the MSTR selling bitcoin market, which was slammed by crypto whales. I played this market a little before Polymarket issued a clarification based on prior precedent, but now I'm slamming full port after the clarification. I have mixed feelings about this whole thing so let's break it down.

## vague rules, hidden precedent

Polymarket has a tendency to write vague and unclear rules which don't cover edge cases. In the case of "MSTR sell bitcoin by..." markets, they allow the markets to be "resolved" and the winners paid right when the time period for the market ends, rather than leaving the market open for pertinent information to come to light. There's nowhere in the visible ruleset that covers this. The only way you'd know is if you asked around or dug up old markets to see how they were resolved, so you'd see the precedent. In this case, MSTR *did* sell bitcoin, but only reported the information after the market should've been resolved.

## the dispute that kept the market open

Except the market *wasn't* resolved, because someone disputed the resolution of the market, presumably hoping that keeping it open for dispute would allow the information that MSTR sold bitcoin to come to light. What they didn't know was that artificially keeping the market open via a dispute didn't extend the market resolution window, but it *did* keep the market open for trading, because for whatever reason that's how Polymarket works. Then we learn the following morning that MSTR had sold bitcoin in that window. A bunch of crypto whales, not really familiar with Polymarket's nuances or procedures, rushed to buy "YES" on this market, because from their perspective they were essentially buying "YES" on the idea that "2+2=4," for cents on the dollar.

But we know due to Polymarket's precedents that's not how that works. I didn't size very big betting against them, because betting against "2+2=4" is always a scary proposition, but knew I was likely to win due to precedent and the recognizable Polymarket whales on my side of the trade, who are far more familiar with Polymarket rules and procedure than I am. Then Polymarket came out and clarified that no, 2+2 does not = 4, because we learned that after the market was supposed to be shut. By their precedents, information that comes to light after the market should have closed cannot be taken into account.

## the 70-cent dollar

The market immediately shot up to 99.9% NO, but hovered between that and roughly 99% while the dispute resolved over the next 2 days. Despite the bolded clarification on the rule page that they were going to lose, a rush of YES bettors flooded in, enticed by the opportunity of 100xing their investment on 2+2=4. I don't feel bad at all for these people, who obviously ignored the posted rules and didn't consider why their stake was so cheap.

But the crypto whales who bought from anywhere between 5 and 80 cents on YES before the clarification was posted? Well, it's more complicated. It was pretty hard to understand how this market was going to resolve if you weren't familiar with Polymarket's precedents, which aren't easy to find. Yet, there should've been a clue from the price, right? No one is offering you $1 for substantially less unless there was some innate uncertainty behind the investment. It would be like a website offering to sell you an iPhone for $5 — I would immediately suspect a scam or read the fine print.

These were fundamentally greedy people who thought they could make the easiest dollars of their lives confirming already known information and got taken for a ride as a result. No crying in the casino.

## except there is a lot of crying

And this is going to cause a lot of reputational damage to Polymarket. For clearly resolving against the "truth," and for taking money from a lot of people who think they've been scammed and giving it to Polymarket whales who know how this usually goes. So I have kind of an enlightened centrist position on this. I think Polymarket sucks for having unclear rules that don't mention precedents like when the market is supposed to resolve and what window of information gets taken into account, but I don't really have sympathy for the people who lost their money.
